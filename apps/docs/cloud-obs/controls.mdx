The **Cloud OBS** page is your home base. Everything you need to run a stream is
here: start OBS, pick a scene, go live, and check that it's healthy.

<Frame caption="The Cloud OBS page: preview, Go live, and your scenes as buttons.">
  <img src="/images/cloud-obs/controls-overview.png" alt="Cloud OBS controls" />
</Frame>

## Turning OBS on and off

At the top you'll see a **Container** section. Your container is the machine in
the cloud that runs your OBS.

- **Launch Cloud OBS** / **Start container** turns it on. Give it 10 to 30
  seconds to boot. The badge turns green and reads **OBS Connected** when it's
  ready.
- **Stop container** turns it off when you're done for the day.

<Tip>
  Turn your container off when you're not streaming. There's no reason to leave
  it running overnight.
</Tip>

If the connection ever drops, a **Reconnect** button shows up. If OBS crashes or
your container gets shut down, a banner appears with a **Restart** button. One
click and you're back.

## Seeing your OBS

The black preview box shows what OBS is putting out. Click **Open OBS** (the
"Click to open OBS" box) to open the full thing in your browser. It looks and
works just like the OBS on your desktop: check your camera, move things around,
build scenes. It's a live window into your cloud OBS.

## Going live

The **Go live** button starts streaming to your platform. The status next to it
tells you where you stand: **Not streaming** or **Streaming**. Hit it again to
stop. That's the whole thing.

## Switching scenes

The **Scenes** card lists every scene you've built, each as a button. Tap one to
switch to it. The scene that's on air right now has a little green dot. While
it's changing you'll see "Switching..." for a second.

This is your main control mid-stream: tap **Starting Soon**, tap **IRL**, tap
**BRB**, tap **Ending**. Done.

## Dropping your camera feed into a scene

Under **Your incoming signal** you can take the video coming from your phone and
drop it into any scene as a source. Hit **Add source**, give it a name, and pick
the scene it should live in. Now your camera shows up in that scene.

Most of the time this is already done for you in your "IRL" scene, so you only
need this if you want your feed in extra scenes too.

## Adding alerts

Want follow, sub, and donation alerts on screen? Add your alert link (from
StreamElements, Streamlabs, Sound Alerts, or wherever you set them up) and it
gets added as a source you can drop into your scenes.

## Checking how it's running

Below your scenes are a few tabs. Two of them tell you how healthy your stream
is.

### Sources

<Frame caption="The Sources tab: every scene and source, and how hard each one is working.">
  <img src="/images/cloud-obs/controls-full.png" alt="Sources tab" />
</Frame>

The **Sources** tab lists everything in your OBS and shows how much work each
piece is doing. You can flip individual sources on and off from here, and turn
effects (filters) on a source on or off. If your stream feels heavy, this is
where you spot the source that's hogging everything.

### Performance

<Frame caption="The Performance tab: is your cloud OBS keeping up? These numbers tell you.">
  <img src="/images/cloud-obs/performance.png" alt="Performance tab" />
</Frame>

The **Performance** tab is the health check for your cloud OBS itself. The big
numbers to care about:

- **FPS** should sit at your target (usually 60). If it drops, your stream looks
  choppy.
- **Frame Time** and **dropped/skipped frames** should stay low. High numbers
  mean OBS is struggling to keep up.

Green and steady means you're good. You don't need to stare at this, just glance
at it if something looks off.
