An overlay is a web page, so OBS shows it with a **Browser** source. Set it up
once per overlay and leave it alone.

## The settings that matter

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| **URL** | Your overlay URL, copied from the dashboard. |
| **Width** and **Height** | Your overlay's size, 1920 × 1080 unless you changed it. |
| **Custom frame rate** | Leave it off unless you know you need it. |

Match the width and height to the overlay rather than resizing the source on
the canvas. Stretching it with the transform handles makes everything soft and
puts your widgets somewhere other than where you placed them.

## The two options that cause most problems

### Shutdown source when not visible

**Leave this off** for any overlay with alerts on it.

With it on, OBS closes the page whenever the scene isn't showing. An alert that
fires while you're on another scene arrives at a page that isn't running, and
it's gone. It isn't queued for when you switch back.

### Control audio via OBS

**Turn this on** if your overlay makes any sound.

OBS doesn't route browser source audio to your stream by default, so alert
sounds and clip audio are silent until you do. It also puts the source in your
audio mixer, where you can set its level like anything else.

It has a second benefit: audio routed through OBS isn't subject to the
browser's autoplay rules, which is what otherwise stops clips playing with
sound.

## After you change an overlay

Changes to a widget's text, position or colours need a refresh before OBS sees
them. Right-click the source, select **Properties**, then
**Refresh cache of current page**.

Live events don't need this. A follow alert fires on a page that's been open
for six hours.
