The editor is where you lay an overlay out. This page is about the editor
itself. For what each widget does, see the widget pages.

<Note>
Editing needs a desktop. On a phone you can still turn overlays on and off from
the list, but the canvas needs a bigger screen.
</Note>

## Moving around

| To do this | Use |
|---|---|
| Pan the canvas | Hold **Space** and drag, or press **H** for the hand tool |
| Back to selecting | **V** |
| Zoom around the cursor | **Ctrl + scroll** |
| Fit the whole scene on screen | **Shift + 1** |
| Back to 100% | **Shift + 0** |

The zoom readout in the toolbar is also a button: select it to go back to 100%
and recentre.

## Arranging widgets

Drag to move, drag a handle to resize. Guides appear as you line things up,
with the gap measured between them.

- **Arrow keys** nudge by one pixel, **Shift + arrows** by ten.
- **Alt + drag** flips whichever snapping is on, just for that drag.
- **Alt + drag a resize handle** crops instead of resizing.
- **Shift + drag a corner** stretches instead of scaling.

Select several widgets and the inspector offers alignment, **Space evenly**
(needs three or more), **Match the first one you picked** for width or height,
and flip.

## Layers

The panel on the left lists everything on the canvas, topmost first. Drag the
handle to reorder, or right-click for **Bring to front** and friends.

Each row has a visibility toggle and a lock. Locking is the one to reach for
once a background is where you want it, so you stop dragging it by accident.

Double-click a name to rename it.

<Note>
There's no grouping. Widgets are arranged individually, and the only nesting is
the clips widget's own caption fields, which are fixed to it.
</Note>

## The canvas view

The eye icon opens settings that are **yours alone and never go on stream**:
the backdrop behind your widgets, a grid, and the rulers.

Snapping lives here too, so you can turn off snapping to other widgets while
keeping snapping to the grid.

<Tip>
Twitch draws its own controls over your stream, mostly along the bottom edge.
Keep anything that has to stay readable away from the edges.
</Tip>

## Saving

Nothing saves on its own. **Ctrl+S** or the **Save** button writes your changes,
and the button tells you whether there's anything to write.

If you close the tab with unsaved work, your browser warns you. Navigating
inside StreamWizard gets a proper dialog instead.

### Picking up where you left off

Your in-progress changes are kept in your browser as you work, so if the tab
crashes you aren't starting again. Next time you open that overlay you'll be
asked whether to restore them.

It always asks rather than restoring silently, so a draft left in one tab can't
quietly overwrite work you saved in another. Restoring still needs a save.

## Changing the canvas size

The size next to the overlay's name opens **Canvas size**. Match it to your OBS
canvas.

When you change it, pick what happens to what's already there:

- **Scale to fit** resizes and repositions everything so the layout looks the
  same.
- **Keep positions** leaves everything where it is, which can push widgets off
  the edge.

Nothing changes for viewers until you save.

<Tip>
**Pinned to** in a widget's **Advanced** section decides which edge its
position is measured from. Pin something bottom-right and it stays in that
corner when the canvas size changes. Picking a pin doesn't move the widget.
</Tip>

## Testing without going live

The **Demo** button feeds fake events to every widget on the canvas. See
[testing widgets](/overlays/overview) for what the Local and Live modes do.
