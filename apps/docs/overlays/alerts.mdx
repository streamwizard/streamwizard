## The fast way: Alert box template

**New Overlay → Alert box**. That's it. The overlay comes with an alert
widget already placed, listening for follows, subs, cheers, and raids.

Click the widget and the right panel lists every alert. Switch on the ones you
want, and see at a glance which have an image or a sound. Only want follows and
raids? Switch the rest off.

Click an alert to set it up:

- **Text**: the title and an optional second line. Click a chip like `{name}` to drop it in. Font, size, and colors sit under **Typography**.
- **Media and sound**: pick from your [media library](/overlays/media-library), choose where the image or video sits, and set the volumes. The sound file and the video's own sound each have a slider, so you can play both, or turn the video down to 0 to let the sound file speak alone.
- **Timing**: seconds on screen, and a minimum (bits, viewers) before it fires.
- **Animation**: how the alert comes in and leaves, and what its text does in between. See [Animation](#animation).

Happy with one alert? **Use this look for all alerts** copies its typography, animation, and media position to the rest.

Alerts queue. If three people follow at once, you get three alerts back to
back, not one mangled one. The line holds 50 by default and skips anything past
that, so a follow-bot wave can't book your alerts for an hour. Change it with
**Most alerts waiting**.

There are 30 alerts, in four groups. The template switches on the usual four,
and the rest are waiting in the panel.

**Reward redeemed** is the one people miss. It fires on channel point
redemptions, so any reward you've set up on Twitch can have its own alert with
its own sound.

### Mod alerts ask Twitch first

Ban, Timeout, VIP added, and Mod added need one more Twitch permission each.
StreamWizard doesn't ask for them when you sign in, only when you turn one of
these alerts on. You'll see why, then Twitch's own screen, then you're back in
the editor. Turn the alert on again and it's live. Save your overlay before you
go: the trip to Twitch leaves the editor.

Twitch calls the ban permission "moderate". StreamWizard only reads who was
banned or timed out, and by whom. It never bans anyone. VIP and mod are read
only too.

Say no on Twitch's screen and nothing breaks: the alert stays off, and you can
try again whenever you like.

A few things to know:

- A timeout is its own alert, so a busy mod team doesn't flood your stream. `{amount}` is how long it lasts, in seconds.
- `{message}` on a ban or timeout is the reason the mod typed, and `{moderator}` is who did it. Neither is in the default text.
- Prediction result names the winning outcome, with its channel points in `{amount}`. A canceled prediction plays nothing.
- Goal achieved only plays when the goal was hit, not when it ran out.

## Animation

Three things can move, each on its own:

- **The alert**: image or video and text together. Pick an **Enter** and an **Exit** effect and how many seconds each takes.
- **The text**: its own enter and exit, under **Text animation**. **Show text after** holds it back for up to 10 seconds, and **Hide text early** takes it off before the alert leaves.
- **The highlight**: `{name}` and `{amount}` in the title can keep moving while the text shows. Pulse, Tada, Wave, and eight more, under **Typography**.

There are 32 enter effects and a matching exit for each, grouped as Bounce,
Fade, Flip, Light speed, Rotate, Roll, Zoom, and Slide. The small tile next to
the picker plays the effect, so you do not need to fire a test for every one.

How the time adds up, for an alert set to 10 seconds on screen:

1. It appears at 0 and plays its enter effect. The enter time is part of the 10 seconds.
2. The text shows after its delay.
3. The text leaves at 10 seconds minus **Hide text early**.
4. At 10 seconds the alert plays its exit. The exit time comes on top, so a 1 second exit means 11 seconds in all.

An effect set to **None** takes no time: the alert is just there, or just gone.

A sound still playing is cut when the next alert starts. Want it to finish?
Switch on **Let sounds finish** under **All alerts**. The next alert then waits
for the sound, up to a minute.

Every variation has its own animation settings, so a big cheer can roll in
while a small one fades.

## Variations

A variation is another version of an alert that plays instead of it when its
conditions are met. A louder cheer alert from 1000 bits up. A different video for
a 12 month resub. One alert just for your mods.

Open an alert, go to **Variations**, and hit **Add variation**. Start from the
alert itself or copy any other alert or variation. Each one has its own text,
media, sound, and style. It is a separate copy: changing the alert later does
not change its variations.

A variation checks one or more conditions:

- **A number**: bits, months, viewers, gifts. Exactly, at least, at most, between two numbers (both included), or the biggest of the stream.
- **Sub tier**: Prime, Tier 1, Tier 2, or Tier 3.
- **Viewer name**: one or more usernames, commas between them.
- **Gifted sub**: on subs and resubs, a gifted sub or one the viewer paid for.
- **Anonymous**: on cheers, gift subs, and gift bombs, sent anonymously or not.

Add up to five. With two or more, pick **All of them** (a Tier 3 sub that is
also 6 months or more) or **Any of them** (either one is enough). No conditions
means it plays every time.

**Chance** sets how often it plays when it matches. 25 is about one in four.

When several match, the most specific one plays: a viewer by name, then the
biggest of the stream, then an exact number, a range, a tier, gifted, or
anonymous, then "at least" and "at most". A variation where all of several
conditions have to hold beats one with fewer of the same kind. One set to
"Any of them" counts only as its loosest condition. Between two "at least"
variations the higher number wins, so 1000 bits plays your 1000 variation, not
your 100 one. Still tied? The first in the list plays, or switch on **Pick at
random when several match** to rotate them.

The play button on a variation tests that exact one, whatever its conditions.

A few things to know:

- Follow, gift upgrade, Prime upgrade, pay it forward, announcement, and poll start have no variations. There is nothing in those events to tell one from the next.
- Modiversary can only match on the mod's name. Twitch does not send the number of years.
- "Biggest of the stream" counts from when the overlay loaded. Refresh the browser source in OBS and the count starts again.
- The alert's own minimum still applies. An event under it plays nothing, variations included.

## Adding alerts to an existing overlay

In the editor: **Widgets → Library → Starters → Alert box → Add to canvas**.

## Testing

Open the widget's code editor (**Edit code** in its settings) and use the test
buttons above the preview: Follow, Sub, Cheer, Raid. Hit **Connect** to test
against real events from your channel.

## Making it fancier

The alert box is a regular custom widget you own. **Edit code** opens the
full editor where you can change the animation, layout, or add more events.
See [building widgets](/widgets/overview) if you want to go down that road.
