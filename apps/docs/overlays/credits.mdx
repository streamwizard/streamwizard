title: "End credits"
description: "Roll the names from your stream when your ending scene comes up: followers, subs, gifts, Bits, raids and more."
---

The end credits widget thanks everyone who showed up. When your ending scene
comes on, it rolls the followers, subs, gifters, cheerers and raiders from the
stream you just did, plus a few numbers like peak viewers and how long you were
live. Nothing to type in: StreamWizard kept count while you streamed.

## Set it up

1. In the overlay editor, open **Widgets → Credits** and add **End credits**.
2. Pick a design, turn sections on or off, drag them into the order you want.
3. Add the overlay to the scene you end on in OBS, the one you switch to before
   you hit **Stop Streaming**.

That's it. The credits start on their own when that scene becomes visible.
They work whether or not **Shutdown source when not visible** is on for the
browser source. Switching away and back starts them from the top.

<Note>
The credits are for your ending scene, not a scene you sit on. Put the widget
on a scene you only switch to at the end.
</Note>

## What rolls

Every section can be turned off, renamed and moved. A section with nothing in
it is skipped, so a stream with no raids never shows an empty "Raids" heading.

| Section | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Title | One line at the top. "Thanks for watching" unless you change it. |
| Followers | Everyone who followed during the stream. |
| Subs | New subs, bought by the person themselves. |
| Resubs | Everyone who resubbed, with their month count. |
| Gifters | Who gifted subs and how many. Anonymous gifts show as one "Anonymous" line. |
| Cheerers | Who cheered and how many Bits, biggest first. Anonymous cheers roll up the same way. |
| Raids | Who raided you and how many viewers they brought. |
| Channel points | Who redeemed rewards, and how often. |
| Hype trains | How many trains ran and the top level reached. |
| Peak viewers | Your highest viewer count. |
| Stream length | How long you were live. |
| Thank-you note | Your own words, a few lines. Blank hides it. |
| Outro | One line at the end. "See you next stream" unless you change it. |

Under **Sections** you can also show counts in the headings ("New followers
· 12"), amounts after names ("sandwichlord · 500 Bits"), and profile pictures.
**Names per section** caps long lists; the rest become one line, "and 12 more",
which you can reword.

## Designs

| Design | Look | Motion |
|---|---|---|
| Classic | One centred column, movie style. | Scrolls up from the bottom. |
| Cards | One section at a time on a rounded card. | Cards slide in, one after another. |
| Ticker | A single line for the bottom of the screen. | Slides right to left. |
| Arcade | Pixel font, square border, scanlines. | Scrolls up in whole pixels. |
| Minimal | Left-aligned, no plate, quiet headings. | Scrolls up. |
| Cinematic | Letterbox bars and big type, made to fill the screen. | Slow crossfade between sections. |

Picking a design resizes the widget to a size that suits it. Arcade brings its
own font and hands yours back when you pick another design.

## Playback

- **Scroll speed** (Classic, Arcade, Minimal, Ticker) or **Seconds per
  section** (Cards, Cinematic). Long lists get a little extra time.
- **Start delay**: the pause between the scene coming up and the first line.
- **When it ends**: play once, or loop. A scrolling design runs all the way
  out of the box and, when looping, comes straight back in from the bottom in
  one motion. Cards and Cinematic hold the last section, then start over.

Viewers with "reduce motion" turned on in their system see every design as a
gentle crossfade instead of a scroll.

## Testing in the editor

The canvas shows the first screen of the roll, dimmed, until you press a test
button in the widget settings:

- **Roll credits** plays the real credits from your last stream.
- **Sample data** plays a made-up stream with every section filled, so you can
  judge the layout before you've streamed with the widget.
- **Stop** puts the canvas back.

These previews stay on your canvas. On stream the credits start on their own.

## Where the names come from

StreamWizard saves the events Twitch sends while you're live: follows, subs,
gifts, cheers, raids, redemptions, hype trains. The credits read that log for
the stream that's running, or the last one when you're offline. A few things
to know:

- **Someone's missing.** StreamWizard has to see your stream start before it
  saves anything. Events from the first moments of a stream, or from a stream
  StreamWizard didn't see start, aren't in the log.
- **Anonymous gifts and cheers** show as one "Anonymous" line per section.
- **Chatters aren't listed.** StreamWizard doesn't store chat, so there's no
  "top chatters" section.
- **Nothing shows on the canvas.** Go live once with StreamWizard connected,
  or use **Sample data** to preview.
